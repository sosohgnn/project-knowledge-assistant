## About these notes

These are my working notes from discovery interviews at Brindlewick Home Goods, held in the last week of January 2027. I was brought in to understand how things are going before Project Lantern (the new order-management system) goes live.

I've written down what people told me, close to how they said it. **I haven't checked any of it yet.** Some of it may be wrong, out of date, or one person's view. Where people disagreed, I've left both versions in.

## Company snapshot (as told to me)

- Homeware retailer based in Leeds, roughly 180 staff.
- Two warehouses: Leeds (the main one) and Bristol.
- Sells online direct to customers and wholesale to around 40 independent shops.
- Orders currently run through a mix of spreadsheets, a shared inbox and an old warehouse system that "nobody wants to touch."
- Project Lantern is replacing this with a cloud order-management platform from Corvane Systems.

---

## Interview 1 — Priya Shah, Operations Director (project sponsor)

- Says the board approved Lantern last spring because wholesale customers were "starting to walk."
- Very clear that Leeds goes live **3 March**. "That date is in the board pack. It's not moving."
- Thinks the project is broadly on budget. "There was some noise about the data side, but Tom's handling it."
- On Bristol: "Bristol follows Leeds. Same system, same year." Didn't mention any change to that.
- Said Elena (Bristol) is "naturally cautious" and will "come round once she sees it working."
- When I asked who signs off scope changes: "Me. Anything that changes cost or dates comes to me."

## Interview 2 — Tom Okafor, Project Manager

- Seemed tired. Said he's running Lantern alongside his normal job.
- The customer and product data from the old system is "much worse than anyone thought": duplicate customers, product codes reused for different items, and addresses in free-text fields.
- Corvane quoted for a "standard migration." Tom says the clean-up is now extra work and Corvane has "mentioned" additional days. He hasn't seen a formal change request yet.
- On the date: "Officially it's 3 March. Realistically? Ask me after the next data load." Said the last test load failed on about a fifth of customer records.
- Mentioned that Daniel (Corvane) floated pushing Bristol into a "phase 2" to free up time. "We talked about it in the steering meeting. I think people liked the idea." He wasn't sure whether anyone formally agreed.
- Keeps a risk log in a shared spreadsheet. Admitted it's "a bit behind."

## Interview 3 — Elena Marsh, Bristol Warehouse Manager

- Frustrated. Says Bristol "always finds out last."
- Her team hasn't had any training on the new system. She says she's asked twice, once by email and once in a meeting. "Both times I got 'we'll sort a date.'"
- Heard "second-hand" that Bristol might be pushed back. Nobody has told her directly. "If it's happening, I need to know. I've got seasonal staff to plan for."
- Bristol uses some workarounds Leeds doesn't, like handwritten pick lists for oversized items. She's not sure the new system handles these.
- Said, a bit pointedly: "Leeds designed this for Leeds."

## Interview 4 — Daniel Reyes, Implementation Lead, Corvane Systems

- Positive and confident. "The platform's ready. The blocker is Brindlewick's data."
- Confirmed Corvane has flagged that the migration is more work than scoped. Called it "a conversation we need to have commercially." Wouldn't put a number on it.
- Thinks splitting Leeds and Bristol would "de-risk" the launch, and says he "recommended" this at the last steering meeting.
- When I asked whether 3 March is still realistic: "It's achievable if the data is clean by mid-February." Didn't say whether it would be.
- Asked me, half-joking, to "keep the report upbeat. These things always look worse in the middle."

## Interview 5 — Ruth Adeyemi, Wholesale Account Manager

- Handles the independent-shop customers. Says complaints have got worse since autumn: late deliveries, wrong items and shops not knowing what's in stock.
- Two of her larger customers have said they're "looking at other suppliers." One of them is the Harrogate shop she described as "our biggest independent."
- She was told Lantern would fix stock visibility "from March" and has passed that on to customers. Now worried about what happens if it slips.
- Has never seen the new system. Doesn't know whether her team is getting training.

## Interview 6 — Marcus Bell, Finance Manager

- Keeps the project budget. Said the approved budget was **£240,000**, including a contingency.
- Says some contingency has already been used on extra licences and on agency staff to cover Tom's normal work.
- Hasn't been told about any extra Corvane migration costs. "If there's a bill coming, I'd like to know before it arrives."
- Doesn't know the cancellation or exit terms in the Corvane contract. "Legal has the contract. I've only seen the pricing schedule."

## Interview 7 — Sam Whitfield, Warehouse Team Lead, Leeds

- Has been in two demo sessions and thinks the new system "looks loads better" than what they've got.
- Said the training so far was a demo, "not really hands-on."
- Worried about go-live week falling right when spring stock arrives. "If March is chaos, it's chaos with a lorry queue."
- Mentioned the old system "falls over every couple of weeks." The team restarts it themselves.

## Things I heard more than once (not yet checked)

- People gave different answers about when Leeds actually goes live.
- Several people mentioned the idea of moving Bristol to a later phase. Nobody could tell me who approved it.
- Training came up from Bristol, Leeds and the wholesale team.
- Extra migration cost came up from Tom and Daniel, but not from Finance.

## Questions I didn't get answered

- Has anyone formally approved the Bristol "phase 2" idea?
- What would the extra migration work cost, and who would approve it?
- What does the Corvane contract say about delays or exit?
- Is there a fallback if go-live slips?
